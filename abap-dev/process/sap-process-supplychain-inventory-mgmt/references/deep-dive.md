# Deep Dive — sap-process-supplychain-inventory-mgmt (BMC)

Read this file when the FS requires real functional-consulting depth on Core Inventory Management (movement type mechanics, special stock, physical inventory, reversal logic, batch/split valuation, stock transfer) — not needed for a quick skill-discovery pass; `SKILL.md` covers that.

## 1. Movement Type — One Key Drives Both the Stock-Type Transition AND the Account Determination

The **Movement Type** (e.g. 101, 261, 311) is the single control key of Inventory Management: it fixes (a) which stock-type transition happens (e.g. unrestricted-use → consumed, blocked → unrestricted, own stock → special stock), (b) whether the posting is value-relevant at all, and (c) which G/L account it hits via **Automatic Account Determination**. That determination is not a lookup on the movement type alone — it resolves through **transaction/event keys**: **BSX** (inventory posting — the stock/balance-sheet account) and **GBB** (offsetting entry for inventory posting — the inventory-change/expense side), each further refined by an **account modification/grouping code** (e.g. `AUF` for goods receipts against orders with account assignment, `AUA` as an alternative used specifically at order settlement) plus the material's **valuation class** (Accounting view of the material master). **PRD** is the separate transaction key used when a price difference must post (e.g. actual cost vs. standard price at order settlement). This is why two goods receipts of the *same material* can post to different G/L accounts — it depends on the movement type and the account-assignment context, not the material alone.

The full standard movement-type catalog spans distinct business intents that an FS often glosses over as "receive/issue stock": `101` (GR for PO), `103`/`105` (GR to/release from GR-blocked stock), `201`/`221`/`261` (GI to cost center/project/order), `301`/`311` (plant/storage-location transfer, one-step), `501`/`561` (GR without PO / initial stock upload), `601`/`641` (GI for outbound delivery / stock transport order shipping), `701`/`703`/`707` (physical inventory difference postings per stock type). Treat "movement type" as a business-process fingerprint, not an arbitrary numeric code — the number tells you which apps, which account determination path, and which stock-type transition apply.

**Practitioner implication:** when an FS says "post a goods movement," always ask which movement type it actually implies (receipt vs issue vs transfer vs difference) and against which account-assignment object — that answer, not a generic "post to inventory" statement, is what drives the account-determination design in the TS. Cross-check with [Skill: sap-process-finance-general-ledger] whenever a new movement scenario needs a new G/L account mapping, since BSX/GBB/PRD configuration is shared FI/CO territory, not something this skill's process owns alone.

## 2. Special Stock Indicators — Segregated Quantities, Not a Flag on the Same Stock

Special stock (indicator **E** sales order stock, **Q** project stock, **K** vendor/supplier consignment, **W** customer consignment, plus returnable transport packaging) is tracked as a *physically and legally separate* quantity from a plant's ordinary own stock, always keyed by an owning object (sales order + item for E, WBS element/network for Q, vendor for K) in addition to material/plant/storage location. The distinction that trips up FS authors most: **own special stock stored elsewhere** (your consignment goods sitting at the *customer's* site, or your returnable packaging at the customer) vs. **externally-owned special stock stored at your site** (a *supplier's* consignment stock sitting in your warehouse). These are opposite ownership directions that both get called "consignment stock" in casual business language:
- **Consignment stock at customer (W)** — your goods, at the customer's location. Not relevant for Requirements Planning. In Inventory Management you can only post the *initial* entry; every subsequent movement (customer's consumption, return) is processed in SD, not IM.
- **Supplier consignment (K)** — the vendor's goods, physically in your warehouse. This IS available for Requirements Planning, and its own goods movements are the ones this skill's movement-type mechanics (§1) directly govern.

Not every special stock type is inventoried or reported identically either: project stock (Q) is explicitly *not* supported by the classic stock-overview function for retail articles, while E/K/Q are all supported by the two Transfer Stock apps (§6). An FS that says "show all special stock in the stock report" needs the report's data source checked against which special stock types that specific view/app actually carries — it is not a uniform guarantee across every inventory app.

**Practitioner implication:** if an FS says "track consignment stock," stop and ask whose consignment — vendor consignment (K, on your premises, MRP-relevant, ordinary IM movements) and customer consignment (W, at the customer, MRP-irrelevant, SD-driven) are structurally different processes with different accounting and planning impact, not two labels for the same indicator. Cross-link [Skill: sap-process-o2c-sell-from-stock] for E (sales order stock) and the project-system skills (e.g. [Skill: sap-process-projectsystem-internal-project]) for Q (project stock).

## 3. Physical Inventory — Book-Inventory Freeze, Cycle-Counting Classification, and Recount as a Document State

The physical inventory process always has three phases — **Preparation** (create the Physical Inventory Document: plant/storage location, planned count date, which materials/batches/sub-stocks, which stock types), **Count** (enter results, optionally via barcode scanning in the Manage Physical Inventory Count app), **Analysis** (recount if needed, post differences) — but SAP lets you collapse them: an **ad-hoc count** combines document creation + count entry in one step; a combined posting can also fold in the difference posting. Three physical inventory *methods* exist side by side — Periodic (whole warehouse, blocked for movements during the count), Cycle Counting (materials counted at intervals driven by a **Cycle Counting Indicator** A/B/C/D on the article master, with a "Fixed" flag that prevents automatic reclassification), and Ad-hoc/Continuous — and an FS describing "count fast-movers monthly, slow-movers twice a year" is describing Cycle Counting classification config, not a bespoke scheduling table to build.

Two document-header mechanics matter for TS design: **Freeze Book Inventory** (`PhysInvtryDocHasQtySnapshot`) captures the book balance at document-creation time so that goods movements happening between the physical count and its data entry don't distort the comparison — without it, a late-entered count is compared against whatever the book balance has drifted to by then, not what it was at count time. **Posting block** (`PostingIsBlockedForPhysInvtry`) is what actually stops further goods movements against the counted stock/stock-type/batch until the count (or its difference) is posted — an FS saying "the system should lock the stock during counting" is asking for this existing block, not new logic. **Recount** is a first-class API operation (`InitiateRecount` at header level, `InitiateRecountOnItem` at item level) that creates a follow-on document referencing the original, not a manual re-entry workaround.

A hard constraint worth flagging early in a TS: the **fiscal year and posting period** for a physical inventory document are fixed by its planned count date at creation, and then locked further by the date of the *first* count-result entry — every subsequent count and difference posting for that document must land in that same period. Also: **blocked stock returns and stock-in-transit cannot be included in a count directly** — they must first be transfer-posted into an inventoriable stock type.

## 4. Goods Movement Reversal — Never Delete, Only Counter-Post, and Respect the Chain

A Material Document is never deleted to "undo" a mistake; every correction is a **reversal**: a new material document posted with the movement type's configured reversal counterpart, negating the original quantity/value while the original document remains on record as an immutable audit trail — this is exactly why this skill's own CDS grounding always exposes both `I_MATERIALDOCUMENTHEADER_2` (full movement history including reversals) and `I_MATERIALSTOCK_2` (only the net balance): a report that needs "what really happened" must read the former, not infer it by netting the latter.

Reversal is **movement-type-specific and configured, not a fixed arithmetic offset** ("+1") — verify the actual reversal pairing rather than assuming one. The Cancel Confirmation API documentation itself illustrates this is also *context*-dependent: canceling a production-order confirmation reverses its goods receipt (movement type 101 in that example) using movement type 131 in that specific confirmation-cancellation flow, whereas a standalone manual reversal of a 101 goods receipt normally uses 102 — the applicable reversal type can differ by which transaction triggered the original posting, not just by the movement type number alone.

Where postings are **chained/dependent**, reversal order is not free choice: later documents must be reversed before earlier ones. The documented example is Advanced Intercompany Stock Transfer's automatic sequence — Post GI from Issuing Stock-in-Transit (GIIV) → Post GR to Receiving Stock-in-Transit (GRRV) — where GRRV must be canceled before GIIV can be; the system enforces reversing "in the reverse sequence of how the system automatically creates them." The same logic generalizes to any two-step transfer posting (§6): the "place in storage" half of a two-step transfer must be reversed before the "remove from storage" half.

## 5. Batch Management and Split Valuation — Two Different Mechanisms Behind "This Material Has Sub-Stocks"

These are frequently conflated in an FS but solve different problems:

- **Batch Management** tracks a definite quantity of material produced/procured under uniform conditions as its own stock-keeping unit within a plant/storage location. Which batch actually gets consumed by a goods movement is resolved via **Batch Determination** — a configured search strategy (a checking rule/checking group combination, analogous in spirit to the stock-determination rule of §1), not a free-text field the user types into. Batch determination is only usable for goods *issues*.
- **Split Valuation** distinguishes and independently prices *sub-stocks* of one material, for reasons like differing origin, quality grade, status, or make-vs-buy. It is configured via **Valuation Category** (the criterion — e.g. origin, or "Automatic (Batch)") + **Valuation Type** (the specific sub-stock, e.g. a plant or a quality grade). Structurally, a split-valuated material has a **mother segment** (the total stock, always **V** moving-average price, aggregating all children) and **child segments** (each independently priced, price control **S** standard or **V** moving average). Every valuation-relevant transaction (goods issue, goods receipt) must specify which sub-stock (valuation type) it affects — only that sub-stock's value changes.

The two mechanisms *can* combine: **Valuation Category X (Automatic Batch)** is the special case where the batch itself automatically becomes the valuation type — no valuation types are predefined; at goods receipt, a batch is created and serves as the valuation type. This is exactly the pattern used in event-based production accounting so that each production lot (batch) carries its own independent cost/variance, visible down to individual goods-issue/goods-receipt/variance-settlement postings tagged with that batch as valuation type.

A concrete functional restriction to carry into any TS involving split-valuated materials: a **cross-plant stock transfer of a split-valuated material cannot use the two-step transfer procedure** (only one-step is supported by the Transfer Stock - Cross-Plant app) — if an FS assumes a two-step plant-to-plant STO flow for such a material, flag this as a hard product restriction, not a configuration gap to fix.

**Practitioner implication:** an FS saying "track cost separately per batch" could mean either batch management alone (physical/logistics traceability — no separate G/L value per batch) or split valuation with valuation category X (a genuinely separate, GL-relevant value per batch). These are configured independently and solve different requirements; confirm which one the business actually needs before the TS commits to a data model, since split valuation changes the material master's Accounting view structure while batch management alone does not.

## 6. Stock Transfer — In-Plant vs. Cross-Plant, One-Step vs. Two-Step, and Stock-in-Transit as a Real Stock Type

Two purpose-built Fiori apps split stock transfer by organizational scope, and an FS that just says "transfer stock" needs to specify which: **Transfer Stock – In-Plant** (App ID F1061 — storage-location-to-storage-location and stock-type-to-stock-type transfers within one plant) vs. **Transfer Stock – Cross-Plant** (App ID F1957 — plant-to-plant, and can create a Stock Transport Order with one click when STO configuration is maintained). The org-level scope, not just "moving stock," determines which app/API and which movement-type family is the right target for a TS.

Both apps expose the same **one-step vs. two-step** pattern at their respective organizational level:
- **One-step** (movement type `311` storage-location-to-storage-location, or `301` plant-to-plant) directly updates the receiving location's unrestricted-use stock in a single material document.
- **Two-step** (`313` "remove from storage" then `315` "place in storage" for storage locations; `303` then `305` for plants) parks the quantity in an intermediate **Stock Transfer / Stock-in-Transit** stock type between the two postings. This interim stock type is a real, reportable stock bucket (visible in `I_MATERIALSTOCK_2`), not merely a UI/processing state — a stock-reconciliation report that ignores stock-in-transit will show quantity as "missing" during the gap between removal and putaway, and (per §4) the "place in storage" posting must be reversed before its "remove from storage" counterpart if a correction is needed.

Special stock types (E/K/Q) transfer only between the same indicator (E→E, Q→Q) or explicitly into unrestricted-use/own stock via a dedicated conversion movement type (`411`) — they are not casually interchangeable with each other. Custom movement types are supported in both Transfer Stock apps, but only after implementing the BAdIs `MMIM_TRANSFER_PROVIDE_DATA` and `MMIM_TRANSFER_CHECK_DATA` on a Z movement type cloned from an SAP-delivered one — an FS asking for "a custom movement type for X transfer" is a BAdI-implementation task, not a configuration-only change. Serialized stock, shelf-life/production-date handling, and split-valuation selection are all supported natively by both apps and should not be re-built as custom fields.
